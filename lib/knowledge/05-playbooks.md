# LEET Client Playbooks

## How to use this
When the FNA data matches one of these archetypes, apply the matching playbook as additional reasoning context — on top of the LEET methodology and house views. The playbook captures how this firm thinks about *this kind of client* — typical blind spots, common fragilities, where conversations usually need to be pushed.

Each playbook is a thinking pattern, not a checklist. Use the framing; adapt the specifics to the actual client.

---

## Playbook A — Young Working Adult (22–28)

> Single, plans to get married, no kids, no portfolio or basic portfolio, building savings habit.

### Defining characteristics
* Income $4,000–$6,000/month, ages 22–28
* Often single or planning to get married and buy a home + renovate
* Many short-term priorities that require cash
* Typical net worth $25k–$50k
* Cashflow surplus easily $1.5k–$3k/month

### What they typically have
* Hospital plans, endowments, or whole life plans bought by their parents — now taking them over
* They may not have started any planning on their own
* Ideal candidate for portfolio optimisation

### What they typically miss
* Tend to save-to-spend or wipe out savings for important milestones
* Lack the understanding of starting 10 years earlier — even a small sum makes a whole lot of difference
* Should consider small 10-year plans alongside saving for their milestones
* Robust planning requires a mature conversation and strict prioritisation

### Hidden fragilities
* Overpaying for older insurance their parents bought
* Whole life policy with high cost and low cover
* Zero wealth-building leading to lost time and compound power

### Cashflow / coordination patterns
* CPF is funding their housing → not much left for retirement
* Believe starting investment at 35 is "not too late" — compounding says otherwise

### Behavioural risks
* Don't think insurance is important at this stage
* Feel cash-in-hand is more important
* Delay tough conversations

### Default product shortlist
* Term plans
* Regular savings ILP
* Hospital plans

## Playbook B — Young Average Family (28–45)

> Having children, mortgage payments, income peaking, lifestyle inflation risk.

### Defining characteristics
* 28–45 years old, family with 1 or more children
* Income: dual ~$18,000/month or single ~$12,000/month range
* Often staying in HDB with potential to upgrade to bigger flat / EC / condo as HDB equity rises
* Net worth including residence $500k–$600k, of which $400k–$500k is property equity
* Easily persuaded into a property upgrade and a family car

### What they typically have
* A term or whole life plan, and maybe 1 regular savings plan each
* Hospital plan for the whole family

### What they typically miss
* Life is in balance right now, but not optimised for freedom or control
* Could work towards $1M liquid investment generating income — but choose to ignore the sacrifices that come with it

### Hidden fragilities
* Optimise lifestyle for current single-income or dual-income state
* Don't account for life-upgrade risk
* Never count enough for life uncertainties — job loss, health scares, relationship uncertainty, divorce
* Plans tend to be about "living today" rather than balancing long term

### Pillar priorities
* Focus on wealth building, diversify aggressively
* Ensure insurance gaps are taken into account
* Consider legacy if finances allow

### Cashflow / coordination patterns
* May save regularly in joint account — but mostly saving to feel confident about upgrading lifestyle, not to achieve freedom

### Default product shortlist
* Shorter-term regular ILP
* Capital-guaranteed high terminal bonus plans
* IUL
* Single-premium whole life plans
* Advisory account (if ILP-sensitive)

## Playbook C — Generation X (career peak, kids on horizon)

> Mature in financial spending, optimises life for the welfare of children.

### Defining characteristics
* Upgrading of house is done — usually in final home (HDB, landed, or condo near good schools)
* No more financial milestones for themselves
* Kids' education either being planned for or already planned
* Retirement plan is simple but short on time
* May appear okay on the surface but could be quite lost about what to do
* May not like to take advice from a younger advisor if the advisor isn't assured

### What they typically have
* Whole life plans, term plan, hospital plan
* Maybe a regular savings endowment that is fully paid
* 1 or more ILP or whole life policies for each child
* $100k–$200k cash in bank
* Asset rich — most money has gone into upgrades and premiums
* Typically feel fully planned

### What they typically miss
* Not much time left to compound towards retirement
* Risk tolerance lower due to age glide
* Mostly reliant on CPF Life, savings, and some endowment plans

### Hidden fragilities
* Will and estate not discussed in the context of overall finances
* Retirement will likely be average — and they may come to accept that
* Most become less ambitious and believe they can live for less

### Pillar priorities
* Children's wellbeing — being able to provide for children, house, savings give them a good life
* Often never consider estate planning properly

### Default product shortlist
* Single-premium ILP
* Advisory account
* IUL
* Single-premium whole life plan
* Capital-guaranteed high terminal bonus

## Playbook D — Pre-retiree / Retiree (55–72)

> Debt-free, transitioning out of active income, dependent on drawdown.

### Defining characteristics
* Debt-free
* CPF at FRS, with some extra money to take out at 55
* $100k–$500k in spare cash
* May consider house downgrade or selling to cash out equity
* Looking for passive income and dividends that last the rest of their lifetime
* Adapting to no income
* May consider a retirement job
* Health may have some compromise

### What they typically have
* Shield plan
* Several endowments that have matured
* Whole life policy with cash value
* Optimisation is challenging depending on health condition

### Hidden fragilities
* Low risk tolerance
* Ability to generate higher yield or returns comes with risk
* Most are vulnerable to bankers and other FAs
* Running out of money in their lifetime

### Cashflow / coordination patterns
* No income — dependent on drawdown from savings
* Drawdown pool typically $200k–$1M

### Default product shortlist
* Single-premium ILP
* Advisory account
* Index Universal Income

## Playbook E — Affluent / High Net Worth ($1M+ liquid)

> Any age. Access to premier or private banking. Wants sophistication — needs simplicity.

### Defining characteristics
* $1M+ liquid net worth, any age
* Access to premier banking or private banking
* May have accumulated net worth from a high-paying job over many years and made smart money moves — including property upgrading in the past (less achievable today)
* Some inherited money early — less aware of it but looking to extend freedom and live lavishly
* Usually want to grow more money

### What they typically have
* Generational endowment plans
* May have IUL or UL
* Hospital plan
* May have large single-premium endowment
* Managed accounts with banks, unit trusts, stock portfolio, real estate portfolio

### What they typically miss
* Simplicity — many choose complexity for its own sake when they don't need it
* Overspending
* Paying high fees unknowingly
* Overall planning for income generation combined with wealth transfer

### Hidden fragilities
* Estate planning not done well
* Reluctance to converse on sensitive topics regarding family money
* Unsure if spouse or children can manage new wealth

### Conversation focus
* Value and principle — why do more when you can do less
* Simple and effective
* Don't fall into the trap of complexity for its own sake

### Default product shortlist
* IUL
* Index Universal Income
* Single-premium endowments or whole life
* Shield plans
* ILP for children, IUL for children
* Trust and will planning
