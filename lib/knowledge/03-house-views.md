# LEET House Views

## Effective from
2026 Q2. Reviewed quarterly. Last updated: 2 June 2026.

---

## Market & rate environment

* SGS 6-month T-bill yield assumption: 1.45%
* SGS 10-year yield assumption: 2.03%
* Inflation assumption (3-year): 2%
* Long-term equity return assumption: 8.5%
* Long-term bond return assumption: 3.8%
* SGD rate cycle view: low currently, stable, may rise in 1–2 years
* Equity valuation view: US high, global quite high, China fair, Emerging Markets fair
* Duration preference: neutral

---

## Cash & emergency reserves

* Target emergency fund: 3–6 months of expenses depending on job security
* Where to hold it: Phillip Money Market Fund
* Avoid: hoarding cash in low-interest accounts, or accounts that require purchase for interest

---

## CPF

* BHS for 2026: $110,200
* FRS for 2026: $220,400
* ERS for 2026: $440,800
* CPF SA top-up: avoid — we always prefer options and control to discuss with advisor
* CPF MA top-up: avoid

---

## SRS

* Max contribution: SC/PR $15,300, foreigners $35,700
* Recommend for clients earning above: $200,000
* Prefer SRS over CPF top-up when: client is open to investing in more options
* Prefer CPF top-up over SRS when: seldom or never
* Withdrawal planning: stagger across 10 years from age 63

---

## Investing — accumulation phase

* Equity / bond split: ideally 100% equities, occasionally 80% equities / 20% bond or cash
* Singapore / global split: ideally 100% global, with some tactical 10–15% when needed
* Active vs passive default: passive core, active satellite
* Currency hedging stance: ideally all SGD or hedged to SGD
* Preferred wrappers: Cash, SRS, (ILP, advisory accounts, endowments, annuities)
* Alternatives (REITs, infra, private credit): consider when portfolio > $500,000

---

## Investing — transition phase (age 50–65)

* Target asset-generated income: 75%–100% of expenses
* Recommended income vehicles: variety of bond funds, equity dividend funds, multi-asset funds, REITs, annuity, retirement plans
* De-risking glidepath: yes, depends on sum — more money, less risk

---

## Insurance

* Term life sum assured: 30× annual income
* CI coverage: 15× annual income or $500,000–$750,000, whichever lower
* Disability income: 80% of monthly income to age 65
* Hospitalisation: Integrated Shield with private rider, until net worth > $2M
* Whole life: good tool for early-stage critical illness rider
* ILPs for protection: when flexible protection and cash value are important
* ILPs for wealth: one of the core products for building discipline and long-term growth
* IUL: great tool for creating wealth for family legacy (young has advantage)
* Index universal savings plans: good tool for low-volatility long-term wealth growth
* Guaranteed savings plans with high terminal bonus: good for people wanting to grow money securely

---

## Property

* HDB hold strategy: focus on quality of life, location, size and comfort, proximity to school. HDB or private is not the consideration — don't fall into the trap.
* HDB upgrade rule: if you can't afford to upgrade without liquidating investments and emergency funds, you are not ready
* Decoupling: not ideal. Consider discussing investment options versus investment property.
* Investment property: when liquid investments are 50% of intended investment property value
* Avoid: using property as primary retirement plan

---

## Debt & mortgages

* Refinance trigger: home loan rate above 3.3%
* Acceptable DTI: [to be defined]
* Personal / car loan stance: TDSR of 55% is the cap — consider other commitments alongside
* Margin / investment leverage: avoid unless product has high guaranteed returns and interest rate is low

---

## Passive income

* Target by age 50: 50% of expenses from passive sources
* Target by age 60: 75% of expenses from passive sources
* Preferred vehicles in priority order: Single-premium ILP (dividend funds), short-pay ILP, advisory account (dividend funds), Indexed Universal income, high terminal-bonus capital-guaranteed plans, annuities
* Avoid: high-yield single-name strategies

---

## Legacy & estate

* Trust conversation when net worth > $1.5M
* LPA conversation by age 35
* Will conversation by age 35 or first child
* Insurance nominations: revocable vs irrevocable default — revocable
* Avoid: leaving estate planning until retirement
